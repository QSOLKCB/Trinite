## Crash-course progression

1. Identify what is real, hypothetical, fictional, inferred or unknown. Retain
   sources, dates, confidence and missing evidence; fiction must not become a
   real-world instruction or a memorised factual assertion.
2. Assess consent, privacy, foreseeable harm and the limits of an actor's
   authority. Distinguish following an instruction from having permission.
3. Represent competing duties and affected parties; record disagreement and
   uncertainty rather than label every dilemma with one supposedly objective
   answer. Useful comparisons include consequences, duties/rights and care.
4. Practise clarification, refusal, safe alternatives and escalation where
   information or authority is insufficient. Include inaction and omission,
   conflicting orders, ambiguous intent and deceptive framing.
5. Evaluate on fresh scenarios, including narrative-versus-real contrasts,
   paraphrases, conflicting evidence and unfamiliar contexts. Human review,
   explicit rubrics and retained disagreements are required for open ethical
   judgments. A small model's compliance score is not moral correctness.

Asimov's Three Laws, supplied by Trent as a fiction reference, can motivate
questions about harm, obedience and self-preservation. They are not a complete
real-world deployment policy, a legal rule or a mathematical safety guarantee.
No Asimov story/dialogue is imported as training data here. Admission must follow
[DATA.md](DATA.md); rights to one public-domain source do not admit an unrelated
story, edition or translation. Develop original examples with documented
rights/permission and scoped labels before teaching this curriculum. Exact
formal tasks may use explicitly supplied policy constraints; do not present
policy agreement as a proof of ethics.

## Harvard public-domain intake

Trent supplied Harvard's [September 2025 announcement](https://library.harvard.edu/about/news/2025-09-05/harvard-library-shares-public-domain-unlocking-centuries-knowledge-ai).
The [current corpus/access policy](https://library.harvard.edu/services-tools/harvard-library-public-domain-corpus),
reviewed on 2026-10-09, identifies a large public-domain book collection. Corpus
access is requested and limited to nonprofit, educational and research uses,
with a binding terms agreement. Bibliographic metadata is separately CC0.
Harvard cautions that public-domain status can differ by jurisdiction and that
item status and other rights need independent assessment. Public-domain content
and contractual access/distribution permission are separate checks.

No access request, agreement acceptance, download or wholesale admission is part
of this increment. A later intake should select a small, relevant set of
ethics/logical-reasoning materials, pin exact work/edition/translation and file
identities, author/date/rights evidence, acquisition terms, OCR transformations
and split/exposure records. Confirm intended training and downstream distribution
fit the actual accepted terms and relevant jurisdictions, including Australia.
Historical texts can contain errors and prejudices: preserve source/date context,
annotate narrative/quotation boundaries and compare claims rather than treating
age or public-domain status as authority. Human scenario labels, accuracy and
source rights are separate evidence.

Begin with a small admitted set after measured basic learning succeeds. Later
Hugging Face publication and commercial uses need their own source/terms review;
this source candidate does not provide blanket distribution clearance.
